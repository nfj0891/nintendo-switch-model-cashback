# nintendo switch: Which Model Fits You, What Each One Costs Now, and How to Get Money Back on the Purchase

Most people typing "nintendo switch" into a search bar already know they want one. The open questions are narrower than that: which model, what it costs today, and whether waiting is going to cost them money.

Two of those have hard answers. The third one changed in 2026, and not in your favour.

Nintendo's US price for the Switch 2 goes from $449.99 to $499.99 on 1 September 2026. Canada moves from CAD$629.99 to CAD$679.99. The My Nintendo Store price in most of Europe goes from €469.99 to €499.99. Japan already took its increase on 25 May 2026, where the Japan-only Switch 2 went from ¥49,980 to ¥59,980. Nintendo's own release attributes the revisions to "changes in market conditions" and the global business outlook rather than naming one cause; reporting around the announcement points at soaring memory chip costs and tariff pressure.

Nintendo apologised for it, which is a sentence you don't see often from a hardware maker. The apology doesn't change the number.

So the real question is not just which Switch. It's which Switch, bought how.

## The four models, and what actually separates them

The Switch family has been a four-way split since the Switch 2 arrived in June 2025, and the older three did not disappear. They also aren't cheap anymore — Nintendo raised US prices across the original line on 3 August 2025.

| Switch Lite | 5.5" LCD, 720p | 32GB | $229.99 | Handheld only. No dock, no TV output, controls built into the body |
| Switch (standard) | 6.2" LCD, 720p | 32GB | $339.99 | The hybrid. Docks to a TV, detachable Joy-Con |
| Switch OLED | 7" OLED, 720p | 64GB | $399.99 | Same internals as standard, better screen, LAN port in the dock |
| Switch 2 | 7.9" LCD, 1080p, 120Hz | 256GB | $449.99, rising to $499.99 on 1 September 2026 | Current flagship. 4K docked, HDR10, Wi-Fi 6E, magnetic Joy-Con 2 |

The spec worth pausing on is storage. The Switch 2 carries 256GB internally and expands only through microSD Express cards — the ones marked "EX". That's a different card from the microSD you probably have in a drawer, and it costs more. Budget for it if you buy digital.

The other thing people miss: the Switch Lite cannot play games that need detached controllers. Super Mario Party and 1-2-Switch are out. If a household has a Lite and someone else has a docked Switch, that's a real limitation, not a footnote.

## What the September change does to your decision

If you're reading this before 1 September 2026, the lower price is still live in the US, Canada and Europe. If you're reading it after, the $499.99 figure is simply the price, and the interesting comparison shifts to bundles.

Nintendo is shipping two bundles alongside the higher pricing this autumn. A Mario Kart World bundle at $549.99, which includes a three-month individual Nintendo Switch Online membership, and a Nintendo Switch Sports Resort bundle at $529.99 with the same three months of online. Mario Kart World sells for $79.99 on its own, so the bundle maths is straightforward if you wanted that game anyway. If you didn't, you're paying more for a game you'll never open.

Accessories moved too. The Switch 2 Pro Controller runs $89.99 and a pair of Joy-Con 2 is $99.99 — both roughly $10 above their Switch equivalents.

Nothing here is a reason to panic-buy. It is a reason to stop treating "I'll get one eventually" as free. A fifty-dollar swing on a console that already costs $450 is a quarter of a game.

## What kills a cashback claim

These aren't edge cases. They're the common ones:

- Using a promo code you found somewhere other than ShopBack. Many codes carry their own affiliate attribution and override the referral.
- Visiting another coupon or cashback site between activation and checkout. The referral goes to whichever site got the last click.
- Ad-blockers and strict tracking protection, which can block the tracking cookie or the confirmation ping.
- In-app browsers inside social or messaging apps. The mini browser frequently drops the cookie. Open the link in your normal browser or the ShopBack app.
- Gift cards, sales tax and shipping. These are commonly excluded from the qualifying amount, and each merchant page lists its own exclusions.
- Buying a console from a marketplace seller rather than the retailer itself. Cashback terms are set per merchant, and third-party listings often sit outside them.

Rates also move. A rate you saw last week isn't a rate you're entitled to this week. Check the store page on the day.

## Which model is actually worth buying

Here's a defensible read, based on the numbers above rather than on vibes.

If you play handheld only and never dock, the Switch Lite at $229.99 does the job for less than half the price of a Switch 2. You give up TV output, rumble, and a handful of party games. For a solo player with a commute, that trade is usually fine.

If you want one console for a TV and a backpack, the standard Switch at $339.99 is now awkward. You're $60 away from the OLED, which gives you a materially better screen, double the storage and a wired LAN port in the dock. The OLED is the one in this lineup where the upgrade price is easiest to justify.

The Switch 2 is the only model with a real generational jump: a 7.9-inch 1080p screen at up to 120Hz, 4K output when docked, 256GB of storage, Wi-Fi 6E, and the Joy-Con 2 that double as mice. It has sold 19.86 million units as of the fiscal year ending 31 March 2026, so the library and the install base aren't speculative. At $499.99 it's the most expensive console Nintendo has ever sold in the US. Only you know whether that matters — but if it does, buying before 1 September 2026 is the whole difference.
